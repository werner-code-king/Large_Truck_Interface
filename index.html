How This Tool Is Scoped

This tool tracks heavy-duty pickups capable of towing in the 15,000–20,000 lb range — the 3/4-ton (2500-series/F-250) and 1-ton single-rear-wheel (3500-series/F-350 SRW) segment. Two towing figures are shown for every configuration:

  • Max Conventional Tow — weight-distributing ball-hitch towing, the figure most relevant to a bumper-pull travel trailer or equipment trailer.
  • Max 5th-Wheel/Gooseneck Tow — typically higher, since the hitch sits over the rear axle instead of behind the bumper.

Dual-rear-wheel (DRW/"dually") trucks are intentionally excluded. A dually 3500/F-350 routinely tows 30,000–40,000+ lbs via 5th-wheel or gooseneck — a different weight class and a different kind of buyer than this 15K–20K tool is built for. Where a family's dually capability is relevant context, it's mentioned in that configuration's notes, but never used as its own rating.

Gas and diesel variants of the same truck are listed as separate rows because the engine choice changes almost every other number in the comparison — towing capacity, payload, price, warranty, and insurance all shift meaningfully between them.

Neither the NHTSA nor IIHS currently publishes 5-star or Top Safety Pick ratings for this generation of heavy-duty pickups (IIHS does not crash-test HD trucks at all) — this is stated plainly per row rather than a rating being invented.

Fields marked est. are analyst estimates — typically because a manufacturer publishes a range rather than a single per-trim figure, or because GMC doesn't publish a distinct number from its mechanically identical Chevrolet twin. All other figures are stated verbatim by the cited source. Prices, incentives, and dealer inventory change frequently — always confirm current pricing, options, and exact tow ratings (via the door-jamb certification label) with the dealership before towing anything near the rated maximum.
